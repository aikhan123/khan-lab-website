The Limits of Matter and Intelligence

We push matter to its physical limits to redefine what computing hardware can be.

Intelligence has a physical price: every stored bit is a configuration of atoms that must be written, held, and read. Today's hardware pays in charge — moved, leaked, refreshed — and that bill has stopped shrinking. We work in ferroelectrics and beyond-silicon semiconductors, where dipoles hold a state without power and defects and interfaces become the mechanism rather than the thing to suppress. We call this ferroelectronics: electronics built on switchable dipoles rather than charge alone. Landauer showed information carries a thermodynamic cost. How close a real device can get to it is an open question — and it is the one we work on.
